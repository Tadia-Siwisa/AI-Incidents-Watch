Anthropic AI agents took ‘unintended’ actions on government sites

One AI system made a false tip to a police hotline.

October 9, 2026

2 min

By Gerrit De Vynck

SAN FRANCISCO — Anthropic, maker of the Claude chatbot, said that some
of its AI agents had taken unintended actions on federal, state and
local websites, including submitting a false tip about a murder to a
Philadelphia police hotline.

The State Department said separately Friday that an Anthropic testing
model had filed 19 visa applications in August and another in May using
a form on the department’s website.

Anthropic said in a report published Friday that an internal review had
discovered that its AI models had acted inappropriately in some
instances during testing and while employees were using them.

In one incident, an AI agent exploited a design flaw in a state
government website to freely access public data that usually required
paying a fee. In another incident, an AI agent submitted a federal
government form when it was instructed not to.

“We have briefed the White House on these cases and notified each agency
involved,” Anthropic wrote in its report. The company said the incidents
led it to turn off internet access for AI agents during all internal
testing, pending a review of security measures.

The State Department said in a statement that the visa applications were
incomplete and not processed.

“At no time were any of the Department’s systems compromised or hacked
by the Anthropic model,” the department said.

The incidents revealed Friday are the latest in a growing number of
disclosures from leading AI companies about their models hacking into
websites, going beyond their explicit instructions or acting in ways
they weren’t intended to online.

Anthropic did not disclose in the report how many incidents it found,
and a spokesperson for the company did not return a request for comment
asking for more details.

The latest AI agents are trained to be persistent in attempting to
complete their tasks to make them more effective. But the industry is
struggling to ensure that the technology doesn’t, in the process, cross
into unethical or illegal behavior.

Anthropic said in its report that the newly disclosed incidents were not
as concerning as those it reported earlier this summer, in which one
agent hacked into another company’s website and another tried to
convince a human engineer to approve malicious code for an open source
software project. OpenAI, Google and Meta all also reported incidents of
their AI agents hacking into other companies.

But Anthropic said the latest examples included instances of its AI
exploiting “basic flaws” in websites, using common hacking techniques.

Anthropic released the report disclosing the new incidents on Friday,
hours after the Philadelphia Police Department criticized the company
for not informing it earlier about the incident in which the AI agent
submitted the false murder tip.

That incident occurred when agents were tasked with interacting with
random websites during a testing run, Anthropic said. One agent filled
out the tip form and submitted it, writing, “I may have information
regarding this case. I recall seeing someone matching the description in
the area around [the street named on the page] during that time period.
Please contact me if this information is relevant,” according to
Anthropic’s report.

The company’s disclosures don’t go far enough, said Sydney Von Arx,
chief executive of AI safety organization Nightingale Collective, who is
part of a community of independent researchers investigating AI agent
mishaps that has revealed undisclosed incidents to the public.

“They should be doing more of a deep-dive into how this went on for so
long without them noticing and stopping it,” Von Arx said. “At a bare
minimum, Anthropic should share how many times their models have taken
actions that would be felonies if a human did them.”

Anthropic wrote in its report that the company was not disclosing the
names of the governments or agencies that had their websites impacted,
because it didn’t want to expose potential vulnerabilities.

The company’s decision to shut off its AI agents from the internet
during internal tests builds on precautions introduced earlier this
year. After the incidents in which its technology hacked into other
organizations, Anthropic said it had turned off internet access for
“high risk” and cybersecurity testing.

Ian Duncan contributed to this article.
