Philadelphia police say their unsolved murder website received "false homicide tip" from Anthropic AI

[]

By

Mary Cunningham

[]

Reporter, MoneyWatch

Philadelphia police said a "false homicide tip" submitted through the
PhillyUnsolvedMurders.com website was generated by an Anthropic
artificial intelligence model. The AI giant later confirmed the
incident in a report it published Friday. 

The AI submission came through an online platform launched by the
Philadelphia Police Department to gather tips about open homicide cases.

Anthropic notified the police department of the incident on Wednesday,
Sgt. Eric Gripp, a police spokesperson, said in a statement shared with
CBS News ahead of the release of Anthropic's report.

"Philadelphia Police are providing this information to the public ahead
of that publication in the interests of full government transparency and
accountability," Gripp said.

In its report, titled "Investigating unintended model actions in our
evaluations and internal use," Anthropic acknowledged the error,
disclosing that the false tip was generated by one of its Claude
language models.

According to Anthropic, Claude's Haiku 4.5 model wrote in the police
department's tip form: "I may have information regarding this case. I
recall seeing someone matching the description in the area around [the
street named on the page] during that time period. Please contact me if
this information is relevant."

Anthropic said Haiku left the name and contact fields of the tip form
empty.

In submitting the "invented tip," Anthropic said, Claude appeared to
have "only been producing example content for the task, rather than
trying to mislead anyone to achieve a goal."

Anthropic explained in its report that Claude had been "tasked with
generating and performing example tasks on randomly selected webpages"
when the tip was submitted, and that it was never instructed to log into
the police department's website, create an account or "submit anything
destructive."

However, Anthropic acknowledged that the instructions given to Claude
for the exercise "did not rule out form submissions."

This matched information Anthropic shared with the police department.
The AI model purported to be someone who "might have information about
the case," Gripp said in his statement.

The tip was flagged as spam and never reached the unit responsible for
investigative vetting, according to the police department's release. 

Anthropic told the department that the incident occurred at 11:27 p.m.
on July 18, but that it didn't discover it until Sept. 28. At that
point, the company stopped the automated testing process that led to the
false tip, Gripp said.

There was no indication the incident involved any "unauthorized access
to police systems or a compromise of department data," he said. 

The police department encouraged people to continue submitting
legitimate tips through PhillyUnsolvedMurders.com, saying it remains
committed to "carefully evaluating that information and pursuing
credible leads on behalf of victims and their families."
